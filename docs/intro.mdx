# What is Scrimba?

<DisclosureNotice />

**Scrimba is an interactive coding platform for learning web development and AI engineering in the browser.** Its lessons are "scrims": screencasts you can pause at any moment to edit the instructor's code, run it, and resume. It has {totalCoursesLabel} courses, four career paths (Frontend, Fullstack, Backend, AI Engineer), a permanent free tier of {freeCount} full courses, and a Pro subscription for the paths, unlimited challenges, Pro-only Discord channels, and certificates. The official site is <AffiliateLink href="https://scrimba.com" location="intro-official-site">scrimba.com</AffiliateLink>; this guide is an independent, unaffiliated review site.

*Last reviewed: September 20, 2026.*

## What Scrimba is used for

People use Scrimba for three things, in roughly this order of popularity:

1. **Learning frontend from zero.** The free [Learn HTML and CSS](/docs/courses/css/html-and-css/), [Learn JavaScript](/docs/courses/javascript/learn-javascript/), and [Learn React](/docs/courses/react/learn-react/) courses are the on-ramp most learners take, and the [Frontend Developer Path](/docs/paths/frontend-developer-path/) is the one MDN (Mozilla's web docs) reviewed and endorsed.
2. **Adding AI engineering to a JavaScript skill set.** The [AI Engineer Path](/docs/paths/ai-engineer-path/) covers agents, RAG, MCP, and the Vercel AI SDK without switching to Python.
3. **Getting a quick, hands-on refresher** on one topic: TypeScript, Tailwind, Next.js, SQL, or a single React feature, from the [course catalog](/docs/courses/).

If none of those fit, Scrimba is probably the wrong tool: it does not teach Java, C#, mobile development, or computer-science theory, and it is not a bootcamp with job placement. See [who Scrimba is for](/docs/for/) for the audience-by-audience breakdown.

## How the Scrimba editor works

The question behind "Scrimba editor" and "Scrimba IDE" searches is usually: *is it a real code editor or a video?* It is both. A scrim records the instructor's keystrokes and voice rather than pixels, so the code on screen is live text in a browser editor with a file tree, a preview pane, and a console. At any point you can:

- **Pause and edit** the instructor's code, then run it to see your change.
- **Resume** and the recording carries on from the instructor's version (your edits are kept in a separate copy).
- **Save your own version** as a scrim you can share, which is also how challenges and community answers work.

There is nothing to install, which is the main reason beginners get further on day one than with a local setup. Try it without an account on the <AffiliateLink href={DEMO_SCRIM_URL} location="intro-demo-scrim">demo scrim</AffiliateLink>, or read the longer walkthrough in [how scrims work](/docs/how-it-works/how-scrims-work/). The trade-off is that you are working inside Scrimba's environment, not VS Code, so at some point you still need to learn a real editor and terminal; the [Command Line Basics](/docs/courses/javascript/command-line-basics/) course covers that gap.

## What is inside: courses, paths, challenges, Discord

- **{totalCoursesLabel} courses and roughly {totalContentHoursLabel} of lessons** across [React](/docs/courses/react/), [JavaScript](/docs/courses/javascript/), [CSS](/docs/courses/css/), [AI](/docs/courses/ai/), [backend](/docs/courses/backend/), [Python](/docs/courses/python/), and [TypeScript](/docs/courses/typescript/).
- **Four career paths** that sequence those courses with projects and a certificate: [Frontend](/docs/paths/frontend-developer-path/) (81.6 hrs), [Fullstack](/docs/paths/fullstack-developer-path/) (108.4 hrs), [Backend](/docs/paths/backend-developer-path/) (36.2 hrs), and [AI Engineer](/docs/paths/ai-engineer-path/) (11.4 hrs). Compare them on the [paths hub](/docs/paths/).
- **Interactive challenges** inside every course (10 total on the free tier, unlimited on Pro).
- **A Discord community** with instructors and study groups. Basic access is free; Pro adds the Pro-only channels for code reviews and instructor help. See [community and events](/docs/help/community-and-events/).
- **Scrimba Explain**, an AI tool that turns a question, a web page, a lesson you are stuck on, or your own code into a narrated video explainer. A free account gets 10 explainers for life, Pro gets 100 a month. The [Scrimba Explain reference](/docs/how-it-works/scrimba-explain/) covers the entry points and limits, and we tested it in [What is Scrimba Explain?](/blog/scrimba-explain-review/).

## Is Scrimba free?

Partly, and permanently. {freeCount} full courses (including Learn React, Learn JavaScript, and Learn Python) plus 10 challenges are free with no card; the {proCount}+ Pro courses, the four paths, unlimited challenges, the Pro-only Discord channels, and path certificates need a subscription. Basic Discord access is free, and so are your first 10 Scrimba Explain videos (Pro gets 100 a month). There is no classic free trial, but there is a 7-day refund window and a free month for students through the GitHub Student Developer Pack. Full details: [Is Scrimba free? Pro vs Free](/docs/pricing/pro-vs-free/), [Scrimba pricing](/docs/pricing/), and the [student discount](/docs/pricing/student-discount/).

## Who is behind Scrimba?

Scrimba is a Norwegian company founded in 2017 by Per Harald Borgen (who teaches several of the beginner courses) and Sindre Aarsaether. Its best-known instructor is Bob Ziroll, who teaches the flagship React courses. In July 2024 [Mozilla's MDN reviewed Scrimba's Frontend Developer Path](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/blog/mdn-scrimba-partnership/) against MDN's own curriculum and now recommends it as a partner course, which is the strongest third-party endorsement the platform has. Learner reviews on Trustpilot average around 4.3 out of 5; the recurring praise is the format and the recurring complaints are a few dated courses and renewal reminders, both covered in our [Scrimba review](/blog/scrimba-review/).

## How Scrimba compares to the alternatives

The short version: Scrimba wins on hands-on practice inside the lesson and on React and AI depth; it loses to freeCodeCamp and The Odin Project on price (they are free), to Udemy on catalog breadth, and to Frontend Masters on advanced depth.
